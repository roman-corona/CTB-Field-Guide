01What the CTB report is for

The CTB report is a working list of shortages for each assigned outline. It tells you which parts to chase and which buyers to follow up with. It is a snapshot, so it goes stale as buyers move dates, schedulers re-sequence orders, and stock changes.

We are not responsible for procurement, but we are accountable for the explanation.
When a part flips from stock to shortage, we get asked what happened. Our notes and archived versions are how we answer.

Goal for every line

Drive every line to Healthy. Anything not healthy carries a status and a comment, written in your own words, that says what you are doing about it.

04Verify in SAP

Reports make life easier, but SAP is the source of truth. Planners and schedulers move dates and re-sequence orders, so a report-only view will miss things.

  1. Open the orderUse the order number from the top row of the column.
  2. Check release statusAn RE suffix means released to the floor.
  3. Explode the component listLook at each component. If every line shows stock, the order is clear to build, regardless of the date in the report.
  4. Confirm the real sequenceIdentify which order SAP has next in line. It may not match the report's column order.
  5. Find the schedulerClick the order, then the magnifying glass, then Assignment. The scheduler may be listed under a production supervisor title.
  6. Ask for the next releaseIf components are in stock and we still owe the customer, ask the scheduler to release the next order. Once released, update the report columns.

05Chase shortages, reds first

Without SAP access, focus only on red cells and start emailing buyers. Copy Fernanda so she can step in if a reply looks off.

Frame the ask as cumulative demand

Give the buyer the finish dates and the total quantity needed. If each order is for 3 and six orders finish in October, the ask is 18 pieces, with the first needed by the earliest finish date. Then compare that to the PO acknowledgement date. A PO acknowledged for mid-December does not support October demand.
